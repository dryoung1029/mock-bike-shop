Short answer: once a year for most people. If you ride most days, commute through a wet winter, or put in long weekend miles, make it twice a year. When I tell customers this, the next question is usually "how do I know if it's time sooner?" That one's easier to answer than you'd think, because your bike tells you.

## What does a tune-up actually do?

A tune-up is a mechanic going over the whole bike and fixing the small things before they turn into big ones. In our shop that means adjusting the brakes and gears, checking the wheels for wobble and loose spokes, looking at the tires, and tightening every bolt that holds you on the bike. Then we ride it around the block.

Most of it is adjustment, not new parts. Cables stretch, pads wear, and bolts settle. None of that is broken — it has just drifted.

## How do I know my bike needs one now?

Here are the signs I see most often, roughly in order of how much they matter:

1. **The brakes feel different.** The lever pulls closer to the bar, they squeal, or the bike takes longer to stop. Don't wait on this one — bring it in.
2. **Gears skip or won't shift cleanly.** Usually a cable adjustment. Sometimes it's a worn chain.
3. **A noise that wasn't there before.** Clicks, creaks, and rattles almost always mean something has loosened or worn.
4. **The wheel wobbles** when you spin it, or rubs the brake pad.
5. **The chain looks dry, rusty, or black with grime.** A clean, oiled chain shifts better and lasts longer.

If any of the first three are happening, don't wait for your yearly service.

## Does riding style change the answer?

Yes. Here's my rule of thumb — it's a mechanic's habit, not a research finding:

- **A few rides a month, mostly dry:** once a year, in spring.
- **Commuting most days:** twice a year, spring and fall.
- **Riding through winter rain or on salted roads:** twice a year, plus rinse and oil the chain often.
- **E-bikes:** twice a year. They're heavier and faster, so brakes, tires, and chains wear out sooner.

## What can I do between tune-ups?

Three things take five minutes and make a real difference:

- **Check your tire pressure** before most rides. The right number is printed on the side of the tire.
- **Wipe and oil the chain** every few weeks, or after any wet ride. Wipe off the extra oil after — a dripping chain collects dirt.
- **Squeeze the brakes** before you roll out. If the lever touches the handlebar, don't ride; bring it in.

## When is it more than a tune-up?

If your bike has been sitting for years, has a lot of miles on it, or had a crash, a tune-up may not be enough. After a crash, especially on a carbon frame, have a mechanic look it over in person before you ride hard again. Some damage can't be seen from the outside. And if your bike's maker has issued a recall, the manufacturer or the U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission is the place to check.
